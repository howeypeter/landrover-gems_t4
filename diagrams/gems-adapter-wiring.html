GEMS K-line Adapter Wiring

Laptop to Raspberry Pi Pico to an L9637D transceiver to the car's OBD-II socket. The transceiver is the whole trick: it bridges the Pico's 3.3 V UART to the vehicle's single 12 V K-line. Pins below are exact, per the ST L9637D datasheet.

  • 510 ΩPull-up, K (6) → Vs (7). Required — the K output needs it to pull high cleanly. Up to 5 kΩ works.
  • 100 nFDecoupling, Vcc (3) → GND. Recommended.

Leave unconnected

  • pin 2LO — L-line output, unused
  • pin 8LI — L-line input, unused

GEMS talks on the K-line only, so the slower second ISO 9141 line (L) isn't wired. TX and RX have internal pull-ups — no external resistors needed on those.

03 Before you power on

Two rules protect the hardware. Both are settled by the datasheet — follow them exactly.

⚠

Pin 16 is battery-live at all times — fuse it

OBD pin 16 carries +12 V with the key off. Put an inline 1–2 A fuse on that tap and never short it to ground. A slip here is a dead short across the battery. (The L9637D itself survives reverse battery down to −24 V, but that doesn't protect your wiring.)

⚠

Power Vcc from 3V3 — never 5V

The L9637D's RX and TX pull up to Vcc, and the Pico's GPIOs are not 5 V-tolerant. Vcc's spec range is 3–7 V, so running it from the Pico's 3V3 is in spec and keeps the RX output at 3.3 V — safe for GP1.

If you powered Vcc from 5 V instead, RX would drive 5 V into the Pico and can destroy it. So: Vcc → 3V3, full stop.

i

It's an SO-8 surface-mount chip

The bare L9637D won't sit in a breadboard — use an SO-8-to-DIP breakout adapter. Pin 1 is marked by the dot/notch; the numbers run counter-clockwise from there (1 RX, 2 LO, 3 Vcc, 4 TX on one side; 5 GND, 6 K, 7 Vs, 8 LI on the other).

✓

First contact: read-only, and prove it on the simulator first

Test any write (coding or Security-Learn) against the built-in virtual ECU first — gems_t4 immo status, coding read — then do a read on the car: gems_t4 live --port COM5. Only once reads are solid should you attempt a write on real hardware.
